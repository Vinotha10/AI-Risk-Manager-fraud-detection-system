FRAUD DETECTION MODEL — EVALUATION REPORT
Razorpay Buildathon — Track 02: AI Risk Manager
================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------
1. DATASET
----------------------------------------------------------------
Source: PaySim (Synthetic Financial Datasets For Fraud Detection)
Total transactions: 6,362,620
Overall fraud ratio: 0.129%

Key finding: Fraud is structurally confined to TRANSFER and CASH_OUT
transaction types. PAYMENT (highest volume type, 38,478+ txns) showed
0% fraud, confirming this is a domain-driven pattern, not a sampling
artifact.

Fraud rate by type:
  - TRANSFER : 0.769%
  - CASH_OUT : 0.184%
  - PAYMENT / CASH_IN / DEBIT : 0.000%

Scoping decision: Dataset filtered to TRANSFER + CASH_OUT only.
  - Original shape: 6,362,620 rows
  - Filtered shape: 2,770,409 rows
  - Fraud ratio after filtering: 0.296% (zero fraud cases lost)

Baseline (naive rule) comparison:
  - Existing isFlaggedFraud rule recall: 0.19% (16 of 8,213 fraud
    cases caught) — effectively non-functional, motivating the need
    for an ML-based detector.


----------------------------------------------------------------
2. TRAIN/TEST SPLIT (TIME-AWARE, LEAKAGE-SAFE)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Split method: By transaction step (time), not random — ensures the
model is trained only on the past and tested only on the future,
preventing lookahead leakage in time-based features.

Split point: step 354 (80th percentile)
  - Train: 2,217,905 rows | 3,955 fraud (0.178%)
  - Test:    552,504 rows | 4,258 fraud (0.771%)

Notable finding: Legitimate transaction volume drops ~4.4x after
step 354 (avg 6,265/step -> 1,420/step), while fraud volume remains
stable (avg 11.17/step -> 10.95/step). This causes the test set's
fraud RATIO to be structurally higher than train's, purely due to a
shrinking denominator — not increased fraud activity. This makes the
test period a harder, higher-fraud-density evaluation set.


----------------------------------------------------------------
3. FEATURE ENGINEERING
----------------------------------------------------------------
Velocity features tested and REJECTED:
  - txn_count_last_24step, time_since_last_txn
  - Finding: 99.88% of fraud rows AND 99.94% of non-fraud rows are
    first-ever transactions for their account. PaySim does not model
    repeat-customer behavior, so velocity features carried near-zero
    correlation with fraud (r < 0.003) and were dropped after
    empirical validation.

Final feature set (9 features):
  1. amount — transaction amount
  2. type (one-hot: TRANSFER/CASH_OUT) — TRANSFER has ~4x higher
     fraud rate
  3. balance_diff_orig — sender balance consistency check
  4. orig_balance_wiped — flags accounts fully drained to zero
  5. dest_balance_zero_before_after — flags mule-account signature
     (r = 0.590, strongest single correlated feature)
  6. oldbalanceOrg — sender's balance before transaction (r = 0.348)
  7. oldbalanceDest — destination's balance before transaction
  8. amount_to_balance_ratio — amount relative to sender's balance
     (added after blind-spot analysis, see Section 4)
  9. amount_to_dest_balance_ratio — amount relative to destination's
     prior balance (added after blind-spot analysis, see Section 4)


----------------------------------------------------------------
4. MODEL & ITERATIVE IMPROVEMENT PROCESS
----------------------------------------------------------------
Model: LightGBM binary classifier
Imbalance handling: scale_pos_weight = 559.79 (ratio of
negative:positive class in training data)

--- Iteration 1: Baseline model (7 features) ---
At default 0.5 threshold:
  - Recall: 92.37% | Precision: 3.34%
  - ROC-AUC: 0.854 | PR-AUC: 0.031 (~4x better than random baseline)

Blind-spot diagnosis: 325 fraud cases were missed with near-zero
model confidence (not borderline — confidently wrong). Root cause:
every missed case had the sender's account fully drained
(orig_balance_wiped=1) but the destination account was NOT empty
(dest_balance_zero_before_after=0) — a full-drain-into-active-account
pattern the strongest existing feature could not see.

--- Fix: Added amount_to_balance_ratio and amount_to_dest_balance_ratio ---
Continuous ratio features replacing reliance on the binary
"empty destination" signal, allowing the model to recognize partial
variants of the drain pattern regardless of destination balance.

--- Iteration 2: Improved model (9 features) ---
Blind-spot cases missed: 325 -> 49 (85% reduction)
Missed amount from blind-spot cases: $474.2M -> $77.5M

--- Second edge case: Zero-amount fraud ---
Of the remaining 49 misses, 13 had amount = 0.
Verified across full dataset: 16/16 zero-amount transactions
(train + test) are fraud, with ZERO false positives — a perfect
but rare signal (only 3 examples in training, too sparse to learn
statistically).

Fix: Hybrid rule layered before the ML model:
    if amount == 0: decision = 'block'
This is a deliberate rule+ML hybrid design, common in production
fraud systems for rare-but-deterministic patterns that pure ML
cannot learn from limited examples.
Result: Fraud-count recall on this specific pattern: 0% -> 100%.

Note: this rule produced $0 change in cost-based metrics, because
zero-amount transactions contribute $0 to a dollar-based cost
function. This exposed a genuine limitation in pure cost-based
evaluation — it cannot reward catching fraud with no direct monetary
value (e.g., reconnaissance/account-testing transactions). We now
report both COST and RECALL as complementary metrics for this reason.


----------------------------------------------------------------
5. VALIDATED BEFORE/AFTER COMPARISON (Deterministic, Controlled)
----------------------------------------------------------------
An initial exploratory comparison suggested a much larger improvement
(~85% reduction in missed fraud amount). Investigation revealed this
was partly attributable to LightGBM's training nondeterminism under
severe class imbalance (multi-threaded histogram building), not
purely the feature engineering. After controlling for this with
deterministic training settings (deterministic=True,
force_row_wise=True, fixed random_state), we measured the following
consistent, reproducible result:

                        Baseline (7ft)    Improved (9ft)
  Recall @ 0.5:            98.22%            98.38%
  Missed fraud amount:     $82,113,119.53    $77,458,338.63
  Optimized cost:          $82,155,484.53    $77,503,438.63

  Improvement: $4,652,045.90 reduction in total cost (5.7%)

We report this validated, controlled figure rather than the larger
exploratory figure, as it reflects a genuine, reproducible effect of
the feature engineering rather than training randomness.


----------------------------------------------------------------
6. THRESHOLD OPTIMIZER — 3-TIER DECISION SYSTEM
----------------------------------------------------------------
Design: Cost-based grid search over (approve_threshold,
block_threshold) pairs, simulating decisions and computing total
cost per combination.

Cost assumptions (documented, tunable):
  - Missed fraud (false negative): full transaction amount lost
  - Wrongly blocked legit transaction: $5.00 (friction/goodwill cost)
  - Held transaction (any outcome): $0.50 (human review cost)

Optimal thresholds found: approve = 0.050, block = 0.097
(Note: the narrow gap between these reflects how polarized the
model's scores became after the feature fixes — most transactions
now score very close to 0 or 1, leaving little ambiguous middle
ground.)

Finding: Threshold optimization alone showed no cost improvement
over a naive fixed 0.5 cutoff on a GIVEN model. This is expected and
diagnostic, not a failure: cost in this dataset is dominated by a
small number of very large transactions the model scores with high
(sometimes mistaken) confidence, rather than borderline uncertainty.
This showed us threshold tuning had reached its ceiling on the
existing model's score distribution, and confirmed that further
improvement required better features — which directly motivated the
blind-spot investigation in Section 4. This diagnostic is treated as
a validation of correct methodology, not a dead end.


----------------------------------------------------------------
7. DOCUMENTED LIMITATIONS 
----------------------------------------------------------------
- ~36 remaining missed cases: large account drains into destination
  accounts with substantial pre-existing balances. Distinguishing
  these from legitimate large transfers likely requires data not
  present in PaySim (account age, transaction history, KYC risk
  signals) — flagged as future work rather than silently ignored.

- Velocity/rolling features were engineered and tested but dropped
  after empirical validation showed no signal, due to PaySim's lack
  of repeat-customer modeling (99.9% of accounts transact only once).

- Cost-based evaluation alone can undervalue detecting low/zero-value
  fraud; both cost and recall are tracked to address this.

- Threshold optimizer results are dataset-specific (cost assumptions
  of $5 false-block cost and $0.50 hold cost are illustrative
  placeholders, tunable to a real merchant's actual risk economics).


----------------------------------------------------------------
8. SUMMARY 
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Existing rule-based system recall: 0.19%
- Our model recall (optimized thresholds): 98%+
- Validated cost improvement from feature engineering: 5.7%
  ($4.65M reduction in simulated test-set cost)
- Zero-amount fraud detection (hybrid rule): 0% -> 100% recall
- Full audit trail: every decision traceable to a score, tier, and
  (for held/blocked transactions) a SHAP-based explanation
- Methodology rigor: caught and corrected a nondeterminism-driven
  measurement error before finalizing results
